# Content review

Reviewed October 13, 2026. Scope: personal biography, career record, navigation, historical writing, and the transition toward an assessor role. This is an editorial recommendation, not an announcement of election results, office policy, or professional assessment credentials.

## Consultant's assessment

The site currently presents a job applicant and a developer's notebook. Visitors looking up Max Quattromani as a prospective public official encounter an old equipment list, a recruiting pitch, and technical experiments before they can understand his career. The problem is substantive: new styling has made outdated claims look current.

Most of the promotional language should go. "Results-driven," "proven expertise," "innovative," and "digital transformation" make the résumé sound more impressive while giving readers less evidence. Claims that Scaffold powered thousands of websites or influenced modern frameworks need independent support. A smaller claim with a named project, role, and documented contribution earns more trust.

The strongest material is already here: building a responsive framework at Time Warner Cable, bringing development responsibilities in-house, leading multidisciplinary development at Placewise, creating reusable components and documentation, and operating an independent business. Explain those accomplishments in ordinary language. Retain the technical record as evidence of a substantial career, rather than turning familiarity with software into a claim of assessment expertise.

A mature personal site can still sound like Max. The history of creating Scaffold for a GLG interview is specific and memorable. The essay about his mother has emotional honesty that a generic leadership biography lacks. Neither requires a joke in the contact description or a list of obsolete browsers. Personality should come from lived detail and perspective.

## Proposed reader journey

The primary reader is a resident, journalist, colleague, or professional acquaintance searching for Max by name. The site should quickly answer who he is, what he has done, what his present direction is, and how to reach the appropriate destination.

Use five primary destinations: Home, About, Career, Writing, and Contact. Privacy and Accessibility belong in the footer. Selected work belongs within Career until there are enough documented case studies for a separate destination. Keep the historical archive distinct from current writing. Do not fill the current section with old tutorials merely to make it look active.

Use a restrained personal biography as the current introduction. Add jurisdiction, role, election status, and effective date only after verification. Once an official destination is verified, give assessment questions a direct route there. This personal site should not collect assessment submissions or duplicate official instructions, deadlines, or appeals information.

## Claims and chronology that need attention

The user supplies a development career beginning in 2007. The current detailed employment record begins in March 2010. Use "since 2007" based on the user's statement, but obtain the missing early history before presenting a complete chronology.

The existing record lists OraLabs roles in 2010–2011 and 2012; Placewise in 2011–2012 and 2014–2017; Time Warner Cable in 2012–2014; GLG in 2014; Qoppa in 2017–2022; Placewise consulting in 2017–2020; and Parsimony beginning in 2019. Consulting overlaps are plausible, but the site should explain them rather than appear to count the same work twice. Placewise Media and Placewise Digital may be historical names; retain the accurate name for each period after confirmation.

Hold the following measurements: thousands of Scaffold-powered properties, influence on modern frameworks, 700 managed properties, 80 delivered properties, recurring revenue growth, 5,000-item inventory, a 40% retrieval improvement, first-year product sales, and email performance rates. These are existing self-reported claims, not evidence-backed results in the inspected material. They may be usable with a clear source, period, definition, and account of Max's contribution. Their omission does not erase the underlying accomplishment.

Do not repeat "Fortune 150" or "industry-recognized" unless the precise attribution matters and has support. Broad leadership titles are better substantiated by what Max was responsible for and how his team worked.

## Factual intake

Before office-related wording goes public, confirm the jurisdiction, exact office title, current election or appointment status, effective date, and authoritative office website and contact route. The draft intentionally makes no current-office claim.

For the career pass, confirm the 2007–2010 history; titles, dates, and employer names; current Parsimony and Qoppa status; degree wording; and the two or three accomplishments Max considers most consequential. For each selected project, obtain a role, date, artifact, collaborator credit, and any supportable result. Ask whether there is relevant public-service preparation, training, or prior administrative experience. Do not substitute a software career for missing assessment credentials.

For the personal biography, obtain the reason for the career change in Max's own words and one or two personal details he wants readers to know. Do not invent community roots, family circumstances, civic service, or motivation. Confirm the personal mailbox is monitored and whether media inquiries should use it.
